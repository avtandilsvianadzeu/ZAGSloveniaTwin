What a Field Value Engineer does

Meaning

A Field Value Engineer links engineering, strategy and the needs of the people who use a solution, often working directly with them. The job is not to present features but to show what a solution brings to its users: time, quality and lower risk.

Software and SaaS

  • a clear case for the change, worked out with the users
  • discovery of the client's processes and bottlenecks
  • tailored demonstrations and proofs of concept
  • tracking the value achieved after launch

Construction and engineering

  • design optimisation on site at equal safety and quality
  • a view of the whole service life: inspections, monitoring, interventions
  • coordination of designers, contractors and asset owners
  • simpler execution at equal function, safety and quality

DigitalZAG sits exactly where the two meet: it is a software product whose content is civil structures, measurements and assessments. Both lenses therefore apply here: the value of information to its users, and the value of decisions over the life of a structure.

Discovery: what the atlas sets out to solve

Explanation

Discovery asks where time or trust is lost. For ZAG's work on structures, the bottlenecks were these:

  • Knowledge of ZAG's work on one structure is spread over reports, COBISS records, project pages and news.
  • A value such as a dam height or a viaduct length often circulates without its source, so it cannot be trusted.
  • The position of a structure is often given only as a place name, not as coordinates.
  • ZAG's work abroad and its international collaboration have no single overview.

Each bottleneck has its answer in the atlas and a figure that shows how far it is solved.

Value case: time saved

Your assumptions, not ours

A Field Value Engineer does not claim how much time users will save. Together with the users, they agree the assumptions and make the calculation transparent. Enter your own values. The starting numbers are only an example.

saved hours per year = users × lookups per month × 12 × (minutes before - minutes after) ÷ 60

The calculation counts search time only. It leaves out the value of a lower risk of using a wrong value, because that depends on each decision.

Service life of a structure

The construction lens

Value engineering in construction looks beyond the start of a project to the whole service life of a structure, at equal function, safety and quality. Good decisions at each stage rest on data with a source.

Constructionquality control, material tests
Inspectionroutine and detailed inspections, dated records
MonitoringB-WIM, structural response, traffic loads
Assessmentload capacity from measured loads
Interventionat the right time and of the right scope

For bridges, B-WIM data show the actual traffic loads. Where the measured load is below the assumed one, strengthening can be smaller or later. ZAG presented the Slovenian experience of this at an ISWIM webinar in 2024. For each structure, the atlas keeps in one place the values, inspections and sources these decisions need.

Background

Where the method comes from

Value engineering was first used in manufacturing during the Second World War. Lawrence D. Miles wrote its founding text, Techniques of Value Analysis and Engineering. The method looks for the essential functions and the best value over the whole life of an asset.

In Europe, value management is set out in the standard EN 12973, adopted in Slovenia as SIST EN 12973:2020. SAVE International awards the Certified Value Specialist (CVS) certification.
